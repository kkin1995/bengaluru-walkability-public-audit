# Greater Bengaluru Authority: Organizational Hierarchy

**Domain:** Urban Civic Governance — Bengaluru, Karnataka, India
**Researched:** 2026-03-14
**Overall Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH (multiple corroborating sources; Act text available; operational details still evolving post-Sept 2025 transition)

---

## Overview and Mandate

The Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA) is the apex urban governance body for the Greater Bengaluru Area — roughly 712-721 sq km of Bengaluru city. It was established on **15 May 2025** under the **Greater Bengaluru Governance Act, 2024** (Karnataka Act No. 36 of 2025), and the BBMP formally transitioned to the GBA structure on **2 September 2025**.

The GBA replaces the earlier Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) as the overarching authority. Its mandate is:
- Coordinate and supervise the five new City Corporations
- Act as the Planning Authority for the Greater Bengaluru Area
- Bring parastatal agencies (BESCOM, BWSSB, BMTC, BMRCL, BDA) under one coordinating umbrella — unprecedented in Indian cities
- Set binding direction for municipal corporations on major infrastructure
- Manage arterial roads, expressways, large drainage networks, and utilities at a metro scale

## Three-Tier Governance Structure

The GBG Act establishes a **three-tier** system, replacing BBMP's previous four-tier model (which had BBMP → Zonal Committees → Ward Committees → Area Sabhas).

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│           TIER 1: GREATER BENGALURU AUTHORITY (GBA)         │
│    Apex coordinating, planning, and supervisory body        │
│    Chair: Chief Minister of Karnataka (ex officio)          │
│    Vice-Chair: Minister for Bengaluru Development           │
│    Member Secretary: Chief Commissioner (IAS, state-apptd) │
│    75 members total                                         │
└──────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────┘
                           │  sets binding direction
                           │  controls funding flows
                           ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│     TIER 2: FIVE CITY CORPORATIONS (semi-autonomous)        │
│  Central | East | West | North | South                      │
│  Each: Mayor (elected, 30-month term) + Commissioner (IAS)  │
│  Each split into 2 zones = 10 zones total across GBA        │
│  Each zone: Joint Commissioner (KAS) + Chief Engineer       │
└──────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────┘
                           │  advisory recommendations up
                           │  service delivery instructions down
                           ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│            TIER 3: WARD COMMITTEES (grassroots)             │
│  369 wards total (final notification: 19 Nov 2025)          │
│  Each ward: Councillor (elected, 5-yr) + 14 members         │
│  7 of 14 members selected by lottery from registered voters │
│  Powers: advisory only (cannot enforce; no binding power)   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Key difference from old BBMP model:** Zonal Committees and Area Sabhas have been abolished. The path is now GBA → Corporation → Ward Committee, not GBA → Zone Committee → Ward Committee → Area Sabha.

## Tier 1: Greater Bengaluru Authority — Detailed Structure

### Political Leadership
| Role | Incumbent (as of Sept 2025) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Chairperson | Chief Minister (ex officio) | CM Siddaramaiah |
| Vice-Chairperson | Minister for Bengaluru Development | DK Shivakumar |
| Executive Committee Head | Minister nominated by CM | Bengaluru Dev. Minister |

### Membership (75 total)
- State ministers holding portfolios for urban development, infrastructure, water, power, transport
- MPs and MLAs from Bengaluru Urban constituencies
- Mayors of all five city corporations (voting)
- Heads of key civic agencies (most without voting rights)

### Administrative Staff under Chief Commissioner
The Chief Commissioner is supported by:

| Designation | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Special Commissioner — Finance | Budgets, fund flows to corporations |
| Special Commissioner — Administration, Revenue & IT | Admin, property tax, IT systems |
| Special Commissioner — Health & Education | Public health oversight |
| Special Commissioner — Climate Action, Disaster Management | Climate, elections, public relations |
| Chief Town Planner | Urban planning, land use |
| Chief Engineer | Major infrastructure (arterial roads, drains, tunnels) |

### Executive Committee
The GBA Executive Committee is chaired by the Bengaluru Development Minister, with the Chief Commissioner as Member Secretary. Its members include the heads of all parastatals:
- BDA (Bengaluru Development Authority)
- BNRDA (Bengaluru North Regional Development Authority)
- BMRCL (Bengaluru Metro Rail Corporation)
- BMLTA (Bengaluru Metropolitan Land Transport Authority)
- DULT (Directorate of Urban Land Transport)
- BMTC (Bengaluru Metropolitan Transport Corporation)
- BWSSB (Bengaluru Water Supply and Sewerage Board)
- BESCOM (Bangalore Electricity Supply Company)
- Bengaluru City Police Commissioner
- Fire and Emergency Services Director
- Deputy Commissioner, Bengaluru Urban

## Tier 2: Five City Corporations — Detailed Structure

### Corporation Overview

| Corporation | Area | Wards | Zones | Zonal Offices | Assembly Constituencies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bengaluru Central | 78 sq km | 63 | 2 | CV Raman Nagar, Gandhinagar | ~6 |
| Bengaluru East | 168 sq km | 50 | 2 | Mahadevapura, KR Puram | multiple |
| Bengaluru West | 161 sq km | 111 | 2 | RR Nagar, Malleswaram | ~10 |
| Bengaluru North | 158 sq km | 72 | 2 | Byatarayanapura, Yelahanka | ~7 |
| Bengaluru South | 147 sq km | 72 | 2 | Jayanagar, Bommanahalli | ~9 |
| **TOTAL** | **~712 sq km** | **368–369** | **10** | **10** | **28** |

Note: The draft delimitation listed 368 wards; the final notification (19 Nov 2025) fixed 369 wards.

Note on cross-constituency splits: Five of the 28 Assembly constituencies were split between multiple corporations (e.g., Rajarajeshwarinagar straddles South, West, and North; Mahadevapura straddles East and South).

### Corporation Internal Hierarchy

```
Corporation Commissioner (IAS, 2-yr tenure, state-appointed)
│
├── Joint Commissioner — Revenue (per zone, KAS officer)
│   └── covers revenue, property tax in that zone
│
├── Chief Engineer (per zone)
│   ├── Executive Engineer — Roads
│   ├── Executive Engineer — Drainage & Stormwater
│   ├── Executive Engineer — Parks & Lakes
│   └── Assistant Executive Engineers (one per ward/cluster of wards)
│       └── Junior Engineers (field level, ward offices)
│
├── Health Officer
├── City Planner
└── Ward Offices (one per ward or shared)
    └── Junior Engineer / Inspector
```

Key administrative facts:
- Zones do NOT have zonal commissioners (abolished). Joint Commissioners head zones and report directly to the Corporation Commissioner.
- Each zone covers one to six assembly constituencies.
- Corporation Commissioners require GBA/state approval before signing contracts or finalising budgets.

## Tier 3: Ward Committees — Detailed Structure

### Composition (per ward)
| Member | How selected | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Councillor | Elected by ward voters | 1 (chair) |
| Citizen members | Lottery from registered voter rolls | 7 |
| Other nominated members | By councillor/corporation | 7 |
| **Total** | | **15 (1 chair + 14)** |

### Powers and Limitations
- Prepare and submit ward development schemes to the corporation
- Advisory recommendations on: tax collection, maintenance of civic services (water, sewerage, waste, street lighting)
- All recommendations are **advisory only** — the corporation is not bound to act on them
- No independent budget authority
- Cannot enforce any decisions

### What Ward Committees Can Do for Walkability Issues
- Document and formally recommend repairs to the corporation
- Create pressure via elected councillor escalation
- Provide documented record for RTI filings

## Subordinate and Coordinated Bodies

These bodies operate under coordination of GBA but retain their own legislative mandates:

| Agency | Acronym | Responsibility | Relevance to Walkability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bengaluru Development Authority | BDA | Land use zoning, master planning, major road layouts | BDA-maintained roads may have no footpaths |
| Bengaluru Water Supply and Sewerage Board | BWSSB | Water distribution, sewerage | Dug-up roads/footpaths after pipe work |
| Bangalore Electricity Supply Company | BESCOM | Power distribution | Utility pole placement on footpaths |
| Bengaluru Metropolitan Transport Corporation | BMTC | Bus transport | Bus stop access and pedestrian infrastructure |
| Bengaluru Metro Rail Corporation | BMRCL | Metro rail | Station area pedestrian infrastructure |
| Bengaluru Metropolitan Land Transport Authority | BMLTA | Transport coordination | Integrated mobility planning |
| Directorate of Urban Land Transport | DULT | Non-motorised transport, pedestrian policy | Directly relevant — nodal agency for footpaths on state roads |
| Traffic Police | Bengaluru City Police | Traffic enforcement | Footpath encroachment by vehicles |
| BDA / BBDMP | BDA | Building approvals | Setback violations blocking footpaths |

**Critical jurisdiction note:** Not all roads/footpaths are under City Corporation jurisdiction:
- Arterial and sub-arterial roads may be under BDA or BBMP-successor (GBA directly)
- State highways in city limits: KRDCL / PWD
- National Highways: NHAI
- Metro station precincts: BMRCL
- Layout roads in newer areas: may still be BDA, not yet transferred to corporation

## How Reports Flow Through the Hierarchy

### Formal Complaint Channels (as of post-BBMP GBA transition)

```
Citizen submits report (photo + GPS + description)
          │
          ▼
  GBA/Corporation Grievance Portal
  - Namma Bengaluru (Sahaaya 2.0) app / web
  - Helpline: 1533
  - Portal: bbmp.gov.in (current GBA portal host)
          │
          ▼
  Auto-routed to ward-level officer
  (Junior Engineer or Ward Inspector)
          │
          ├─── Routine issue (pothole, broken footpath tile, missing railing)
          │    └── Junior Engineer → Assistant Executive Engineer (AEE)
          │        └── Resolved or escalated to Executive Engineer
          │
          ├─── Major infrastructure failure (collapsed drain, missing slab)
          │    └── AEE → Executive Engineer → Chief Engineer (zone)
          │            └── Corporation Commissioner if budget > threshold
          │
          └─── Parastatal jurisdiction (BWSSB pipe damage, BESCOM pole)
               └── Routed to respective agency by corporation
                   └── BWSSB / BESCOM internal complaint system
```

### Escalation Path
1. **Ward level** — Junior Engineer / AEE (first point of resolution; ~3-7 day SLA)
2. **Zonal level** — Joint Commissioner / Chief Engineer (if unresolved)
3. **Corporation level** — Commissioner (systemic issues, large spend)
4. **GBA level** — Chief Commissioner (cross-corporation or major arterial issues)
5. **State level** — Public Grievance Officer / IPGRS portal (if still unresolved)

### Issue Routing by Type

| Issue Type | Primary Responsible Body | Secondary / Escalation |
|---|---|---|
| Broken footpath tiles on ward road | City Corporation (Ward office → AEE) | Corporation Commissioner |
| Missing footpath on arterial road | GBA Chief Engineer or BDA | GBA / KRDCL |
| Encroachment blocking footpath | Corporation Enforcement + Traffic Police | Commissioner |
| Open manhole / drain slab | Corporation Engineering | BWSSB if sewer drain |
| Footpath dug up by BWSSB work | BWSSB (restoration obligation) | Corporation to chase |
| Street light missing on footpath | BESCOM | Corporation to coordinate |
| Pedestrian signal not working | Traffic Police + BBMP IT | Corporation |
| BDA layout road with no footpath | BDA | DULT for policy |
| Tree blocking footpath | Corporation Horticulture | Corporation |

## Old BBMP Structure vs New GBA Structure (Comparison)

| Aspect | BBMP (pre-Sept 2025) | GBA (post-Sept 2025) |
|---|---|---|
| Apex body | BBMP (single corporation) | Greater Bengaluru Authority |
| Tiers | 4 (BBMP → Zonal → Ward → Area Sabha) | 3 (GBA → Corporation → Ward Committee) |
| Municipal corporations | 1 (BBMP covering whole city) | 5 (Central, East, West, North, South) |
| Total wards | 198 (BBMP) then 243 | 369 (final, Nov 2025) |
| Zones | 8 zonal committees with Zonal Commissioners | 10 zones; Joint Commissioners report to Corp. Commissioner |
| Mayor tenure | 1 year | 30 months |
| Area Sabhas | Existed under BBMP Act 2020 | Abolished in GBA Act |
| Parastatal coordination | Agencies independent | All under GBA Executive Committee |
| Planning authority | No unified MPC | GBA acts as Planning Authority |
| CM involvement | Low (BBMP was autonomous ULB) | High — CM chairs GBA |

## Relevance to the Bengaluru Walkability Public Audit App

### Why This Hierarchy Matters for Report Routing

The walkability audit app collects geo-tagged photos of subpar pedestrian infrastructure. To maximize impact, each report needs to be attributable to the correct responsible body. The hierarchy above informs three key decisions:

**1. Ward Attribution**
Every submission should be mapped to a ward using GPS coordinates. The GBA's 369 wards (final, Nov 2025) are the atomic unit for routing. The "Know Your Corporation" tool at `bbmp.gov.in/KnowYourNewCorporation/index.html` confirms ward from address, and the GBA GIS Viewer provides boundary polygons available as open data.

**2. Corporation Attribution**
Each ward belongs to exactly one of the five corporations. Complaints need to reference the correct Corporation Commissioner's office. This is the body with actual executive power over ward-level roads and footpaths.

**3. Parastatal Escalation**
If the issue is on an arterial road (GBA direct jurisdiction), a state highway (KRDCL/PWD), a BDA layout, or caused by a parastatal (BWSSB trench, BESCOM pole), the report should flag this so it routes outside the corporation system.

### Practical Report Routing Logic

```
GPS coordinates of issue
        │
        ▼
Lookup ward from GBA ward boundary GeoJSON
        │
        ├─ Ward identified? YES → assign ward number + corporation
        │       │
        │       └─ Road type classification:
        │           ├─ Ward/division road → Corporation AEE (ward office)
        │           ├─ Arterial/sub-arterial → GBA Engineering / BDA
        │           └─ State highway in city → KRDCL/PWD
        │
        └─ Ward not identified (outside 721 sq km) → outside GBA jurisdiction
```

### Key Contact Tiers for Reports

| Tier | Who | When to contact |
|---|---|---|
| Ward | Elected Councillor + Ward Committee | Initial report; community pressure |
| Zonal | Joint Commissioner (one per zone) | Unresolved after 7 days |
| Corporation | Commissioner (one per corporation) | Systemic / structural issues |
| GBA | Chief Commissioner | Cross-corporation; arterial roads |
| DULT | Director | Footpath policy; non-motorised transport |
| State | IPGRS / CM Helpline | Final escalation |

### Limitations and Gaps

1. **No formal grievance mechanism in the Act** — The GBG Act does not mandate a complaint redressal system. The existing Namma Bengaluru / Sahaaya 2.0 app is an operational carryover from BBMP, not a statutory right. Its continuation under GBA post-Sept 2025 should be verified.

2. **Ward elections pending** — As of the Act's passage, ward councillor elections had not been held. Ward Committees are not yet fully constituted, limiting their functioning.

3. **Jurisdiction ambiguity persists** — Multiple agencies (BDA, KRDCL, BMRCL, NHAI, corporations) own different stretches of road within the same city. The app needs a road type/jurisdiction field or it risks reports going to the wrong body.

4. **Power concentrated at GBA/state level** — The Act allows the state to alter corporation boundaries at any time and routes all significant financial decisions through GBA. This means the effective decision-maker for a major footpath project is not the ward councillor but the corporation commissioner or higher.

5. **Ward boundaries are new** — The 369-ward delimitation was notified November 2025. Older BBMP ward boundaries (198 or 243 wards) are now obsolete for routing. Ensure GeoJSON data sources are post-Nov-2025.
